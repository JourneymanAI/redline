I'm building a product called Redline. It reads a contract, lease, freelance
agreement or terms of service and tells the reader what they're actually
signing: a plain-English summary, risky clauses ranked by severity with the
exact source sentence shown, a drafted counter-offer for each one, and a
question box that answers only from the document.
That is a hypothesis, not a validated product. Before I write a PRD I want it
anchored in real pain.
Run four research agents IN PARALLEL. Use the Sonnet model for every one of
them. Do not do the research yourself in this session.
Agent 1, who has this pain: find real people describing being hurt by contract
terms they did not understand or did not notice. Public forums and communities.
Capture verbatim quotes and where each one came from.
Agent 2, what goes wrong: identify the recurring clause types that actually
burn people. Auto-renewal, unilateral termination, IP assignment, liability
caps, indemnity, non-competes, fee escalators, and whatever else the evidence
shows. Rank them by how often they show up in complaints.
Agent 3, what already exists: find existing contract-review and clause-analysis
products. For each one, what it does, who it targets, what it charges, and the
most common complaint about it. Include both free and AI-native ones.
Agent 4, who would pay: identify the segments feeling this most sharply, and
any evidence about willingness to pay. Stated prices, what people currently
spend on a lawyer for this, what they call too expensive.
Guardrails, for every agent:- Hard cap of 12 web searches and 15 pages read. Stop at whichever comes first.- Stop early once you have 8 distinct sourced findings. More is not better.- Every finding carries a source URL. If you cannot source it, drop it.- Do not speculate to fill gaps. Say what you could not find.- Write only to research/<agent-name>.md. No code, no other files.
When all four are done, write research/summary.md with:- The three sharpest pain points, each with a real quote and its source- The clause types that matter most, ranked- Where the existing tools are weak- Who would plausibly pay, and roughly what- Anything that contradicts my hypothesis. If the evidence does not support
building this, say so plainly.
Then stop and tell me you're done. Do not start designing or building anything.
